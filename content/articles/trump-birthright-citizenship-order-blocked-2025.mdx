# Trump’s Birthright Citizenship Order Blocked: Here’s What We Know So Far  

**By Surbhi Gloria Singh – Business Standard**  
**Updated:** Jan 24, 2025 • **New Delhi**  

---

## Judge Rules Trump’s Order “Blatantly Unconstitutional”  

In a relief to Indian tech professionals and green card applicants, a **US federal judge has blocked Donald Trump’s order** restricting automatic birthright citizenship.  

US District Judge **John Coughenour** in Seattle issued a **temporary restraining order** after four Democratic-led states—Washington, Arizona, Illinois, and Oregon—challenged the executive order signed by Trump on his first day back in office.  

> “I am having trouble understanding how a member of the bar could state unequivocally that this order is constitutional. This is a blatantly unconstitutional order.”  
> — Judge Coughenour  

The order had directed federal agencies to deny citizenship to children born in the US if neither parent was a citizen or lawful permanent resident.  

Washington Attorney General **Nick Brown** welcomed the ruling, saying:  
> “You are an American citizen if you were born on American soil—period. Nothing that the president can do will change that.”  

---

## What Was at Stake?  

- Over **150,000 children born annually** could have been denied citizenship.  
- Six lawsuits have been filed, including challenges by **22 states** and civil rights groups.  
- The next major hearing is scheduled for **February 6, 2025**.  

Trump responded to the ruling saying:  
> “Obviously we’ll appeal.”  

---

## Impact on Indian Families  

The executive order caused **widespread anxiety** among Indian families in the US, especially those on:  

- **H-1B (work visas)**  
- **H-4 (dependent visas)**  
- **L (intra-company transfer visas)**  
- **F (student visas)**  

> “With over 4–5 million Indian Americans residing in the US, this executive order could have severe consequences for their children and future generations.”  
> — Vishal Gehrana, Advocate, Karanjawala & Co  

### Risks for Indian Families in Backlogs  

- Without automatic citizenship, children could lose access to **in-state tuition, scholarships, and financial aid**.  
- Families waiting decades in green card queues faced the prospect of their children self-deporting at 21.  
- Even lawful temporary residents would have been denied automatic citizenship.  

> “This order adds another layer of stress for families already facing barriers to permanent residency.”  
> — Varun Singh, MD, XIPHIAS Immigration  

---

## Economic and Social Ripple Effects  

Legal experts warn the impact would go beyond families:  

- **Healthcare, research, and education** sectors reliant on immigrant workers could suffer.  
- Maternity clinics reported a surge in requests for **preterm deliveries** (C-sections) as Indian mothers rushed to give birth before the order’s February 20 implementation date.  

> “This could have a ripple effect on the US economy, as these industries are heavily dependent on immigrant workers.”  
> — Smita Paliwal, Partner, King Stubb & Kasiva  

---

## The Bigger Picture  

- Over **1 million Indians** remain stuck in employment-based green card backlogs.  
- Trump’s order specifically excluded children of parents on **temporary visas** from citizenship.  
- The ruling provides **temporary relief**, but the legal fight is far from over.  

> “There is an urgent need for legislative reform to address green card backlogs and streamline immigration processes. Immigrants who contribute to the US economy should not be left in prolonged uncertainty.”  
> — Varun Singh
