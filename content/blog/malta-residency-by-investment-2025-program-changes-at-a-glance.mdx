Malta has long been a sought-after destination for global investors, retirees, and families looking for European residency without the need to relocate full-time. With the implementation of **Legal Notice 146 of 2025**, the country has introduced several **key reforms** to its **[Malta Permanent Residence Program (MPRP)](https://www.xiphiasimmigration.com/malta-citizenship-by-investment.html)**, aimed at making it more efficient, transparent, and appealing, especially for families and property investors.

Lets take a detailed look at the key updates to Maltas Permanent Residence Program, and why theyre a game-changer for investors, families, and global residents alike.

## 1\. Temporary One-Year Residence Permit: Start Living in Malta Sooner

One of the most significant updates is the introduction of a **temporary one-year residence permit**. Under the revised rules, applicants and their eligible family members can receive this temporary permit **as soon as they pass the initial due diligence stage**.

### Why is this important?

Previously, applicants had to wait several months for full approval before being able to reside in Malta. Now, with this permit, they can **legally live in Malta almost immediately** after their preliminary approval. This is especially beneficial for families with children who want to enroll in schools, or retirees looking to start enjoying Maltas lifestyle without delay.

If the application is ultimately rejected, the permit is revoked within 15 days, ensuring regulatory integrity.

## 4\. New Leasing Rights: Earn Income from Your Investment

For applicants who choose to **purchase a qualifying property** in Malta, the programme now allows the option to **lease the property out** if they are not residing in the country.

**Why does this matter?**Previously, strict rules prohibited renting out the investment property. Now, you can generate **rental income**, which helps offset your investment cost. This is especially appealing for those who intend to maintain residency for travel and business purposes but dont plan to live in Malta full-time.

Going forward, anyone assisting applicants, be it agents, consultants, or legal advisors, must hold a **valid annual license** from the responsible Maltese authority. They must also pass thorough **due diligence checks**.
